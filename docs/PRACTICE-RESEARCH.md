# Research behind the practice algorithm (2026-11-15)

Three research passes (motor learning, tempo methods, song sectioning). Evidence is small-sample and mostly piano/winds; none is guitar-specific. Treat numbers as defaults.

| Claim | Strength | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Share of correct attempts, not time or reps, predicts next-day performance; top practicers stop before errors and fix the exact spot | Study (N=17, correlational; via summaries) | Duke, Simmons & Cash 2009 ([summary](https://musicmark.org.uk/news/8-things-top-practicers-do-differently)) |
| Experts shrink the unit only as needed, succeed in 1–2 tries, then go back up; rarely 2 failed attempts in a row | Observational | [Killion & Duke 2025](https://journalofexpertise.org/articles/volume8_issue2-3/JoE_8_2-3_Killion_Duke.pdf) |
| Practice at section boundaries; hard bars get more reps; final sessions are run-throughs | Case study | [Chaffin et al. 2003](https://musiclab.uconn.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/290/2013/10/Seeing-the-Big-Picture-Piano-Practice-as-Expert-Chaffin-2003.pdf) |
| In-session performance misleads; retention must be tested later | Strong review | [Soderstrom & Bjork 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25910388/) |
| Interleaving helps retention; in music the effect is mixed/small | Moderate (motor), weak (music) | [Carter & Grahn 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01251/full) |
| Sleep protects motor skills; whether it improves them is disputed; new material right after can cancel consolidation | Moderate / contested | [Walker 2002](https://walkerlab.berkeley.edu/reprints/Walker%20et%20al._Neuron_2002.pdf), [Rickard 2008](https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/sleep-does-not-enhance-motor-sequence-learning/) |
| The "85% rule" is from a gradient-descent model; for instruments only "moderate difficulty" is supported | Weak for the number | [Wilson 2019](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/0v22s4g4), challenge-point framework |
| Early skill gains appear during short rests between attempts | Moderate | [Bönstrup 2019](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/nitzancensor/files/2022/02/BonstrupCensor2019.pdf) |
| Motor skills fade slowly (half of gains lost ~6.5–13 months without practice) | Meta-analysis | [2025 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40455501/) |
| Very slow practice can train a different motion than fast playing; find the tempo where the fast motion appears | Expert (video analysis) | [Troy Grady](https://troygrady.com/?p=219374), [Kageyama](https://bulletproofmusician.com/is-slow-practice-really-necessary/) |
| Bursts / top-down practice, overspeed, interleaved clicking up, rhythm variations | Expert consensus, no controlled study | [Gebrian list](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/62d41f4e5352371c9d792542/t/63c448dbb5a26930920aaa93/1673808093002/The+Amazing+List+of+Practice+Techniques+with+Gingold+rhythms.pdf), [Sulliman](https://bulletproofmusician.com/jason-sulliman-on-why-fast-practice-can-be-more-efficient-and-effective-than-slow-practice/) |
| Part vs whole: no overall difference; part helps complex tasks with loosely linked parts | Meta-analysis (abstract) | [Whole vs part](https://www.bohrium.com/en/paper-details/whole-and-part-practice-a-meta-analysis/811736563548422146-6897) |
| Forward vs backward chaining in music | No study found (opinion) | — |
| Myelin "deep practice" and "10,000 hours" claims | Overstated | [Macnamara 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4101876) |
